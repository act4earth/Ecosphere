How to run the chromatography lab

  1. Plant material. Choose a leaf. Its pigments are extracted by grinding it with a little sand and acetone (propanone).
  2. Origin line. Draw the origin line across the paper, about 2 cm from the bottom, in pencil. Graphite does not dissolve; ink does.
  3. Load. Apply the extract along the line (or as one spot) with a capillary tube. Let each application dry before the next, so the starting band stays narrow. Several applications make the faint pigments visible; too many overload the paper and the bands streak.
  4. Solvent. The solvent must be below the origin line, or the pigments dissolve into it. Keep the lid on so the air inside is saturated with vapour and the front rises evenly.
  5. Run. The solvent rises up the paper by capillary action, fast at first and then more slowly. Take the paper out when the front is 1–2 cm from the top.
  6. Mark the front at once (✏ Mark the front, then click on the paper where the wet edge is). It evaporates within seconds.
  7. Measure from the origin line to the solvent front and to the centre of each band, in cm (to the nearest 0.1 cm, i.e. 1 mm). Drag the ruler so 0 is on the origin line, and hover for the magnifier.
  8. Calculate Rf = distance moved by the pigment ÷ distance moved by the solvent front, then identify each band from its colour and Rf (reference table below the results).
